Before You Pay That Ticket

The bill arrives in three parts.

Only the first one is printed on the summons. New York raised its point values on February 16, 2026, so the other two are larger than they were.

The fine is the number you can see. The other two find you later.

01

The points

They attach to the date you were stopped, not the date you were convicted, and they sit on your total for 24 months. Reach 11 inside that window and the DMV can take your license.

  • Mobile phone or device5
  • Passing a stopped school bus8

02

The DMV assessment

Six points in eighteen months and the Department of Motor Vehicles charges you separately from anything the court ordered. It is billed every year for three years, and your license is suspended if it goes unpaid. It arrives months after you thought the matter was closed.

03

Your insurance

An insurer is permitted to reprice you after a conviction, and the increase can follow you for years after the ticket is paid. For most drivers this is the largest of the three, and the one nobody budgets for.

Point values and the 24 month lookback are taken from the New York State Department of Motor Vehicles driver point system. Current as of the date on this page, given as general information, not as legal advice about your case.
