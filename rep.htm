What changed on February 16, 2026

New York raised the point value of several violations and lengthened the period over which points are counted. Any chart published before that date, including the one this page replaces, now understates what a conviction costs you.

The lookback moved from 18 months to 24. Alcohol and drug related incidents, aggravated unlicensed operation, work zone speeding, bridge strikes, leaving the scene of a personal injury accident, failure to exercise due care, facilitating aggravated unlicensed operation and speed contests all now carry points that they did not carry before.

How the total works

Points attach to the date of the violation, not the conviction, so a ticket left sitting still lands on the day you were stopped. They count toward your total for 24 months.

  • 11 points in 24 months and the DMV may suspend your license
  • Three speeding convictions inside the counting period can cost you your license on their own, whatever the total says
  • 6 points in 18 months and a separate Driver Responsibility Assessment is charged on top of every court fine

A plea that drops the points is usually worth more than a plea that drops the fine.

The Driver Responsibility Assessment

This is a Department of Motor Vehicles charge, separate from anything the court imposes. Six points inside eighteen months triggers it, and so do alcohol and drug related convictions and chemical test refusals in their own right.

It is billed annually for three years, and your license, permit or driving privilege is suspended if it goes unpaid. Because it comes from the DMV rather than the court, it reaches people who believed the matter had finished months earlier.

The point of this page

The fine on the ticket is rarely the real cost. The assessment, the premium and the risk to your license are. All three are decided by the points, not by the amount you hand over at the window.

Where these figures come from

The point values and the 24 month lookback on this page are taken from the New York State Department of Motor Vehicles driver point system, and from the department's own notice of the values that became enforceable on February 16, 2026.

This is general information about New York law. It is not legal advice about your case, and the charge written on your ticket may not carry the point value you expect. Check it before you pay anything.
