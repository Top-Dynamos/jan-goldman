Practice Areas

Four areas, handled from one Garden City office. If your matter falls outside them you will be told so on the first call, rather than passed around.

Traffic Violations

Speeding, mobile phone and portable device tickets, stop sign and signal violations, improper passing and lane changes, school bus and work zone charges, and the DMV hearings that follow them. Matters are handled in Nassau and Suffolk, and in the New York City Traffic Violations Bureau, where the rules are not the same.

Paying the ticket is pleading guilty

A summons is an accusation, not a finding. Paying it ends the inconvenience and begins the consequence: a conviction on your driving record, points against your license, and a figure your insurer is entitled to act on. Two or three ordinary tickets can carry enough points to put your license at risk, and a single serious charge can do it on its own.

Worth knowing

Points are counted from the date of the violation, not the date you are convicted, and they stay on your total for 24 months. Reaching 11 in that window exposes you to a DMV suspension hearing.

What can actually be done

Most of the work happens before anyone stands up in court. The charge is read properly, the paperwork is checked, and where there is a defence it is prepared rather than improvised.

  • Appearing for you where the court permits it, so you are not losing a day's pay to a traffic matter
  • Negotiating, where the court and the prosecutor allow, toward a plea that carries fewer points or none
  • Challenging the charge on the evidence when the evidence does not hold
  • Representing you at a DMV hearing when your license is already at risk

No outcome can be promised in advance, by this office or any other. What can be promised is that you will be told early which outcomes are realistic.

Driving While Intoxicated

Driving while intoxicated, driving while ability impaired, and refusal of a chemical test. These are charges where the first few days matter, and where people often make the situation worse by waiting.

You are facing two cases, not one

There is the criminal case in court, and there is a separate Department of Motor Vehicles proceeding that follows a chemical test refusal. The DMV matter runs on its own track and can cost you your license regardless of what happens in the courtroom. Handling one and ignoring the other is how people end up acquitted and unable to drive.

Before you say anything

What you tell an officer, an insurer or a prosecutor cannot be taken back later. Say that you want to speak to an attorney, and then do it.

What this office looks at

  • Whether the stop itself was lawful
  • How the roadside testing was conducted and recorded
  • The maintenance and calibration record of any instrument used
  • What was said, when it was said, and whether it should be admissible
  • The licensing consequences of every plea on the table, not only the criminal ones

Criminal Matters

Misdemeanour and felony defence in the Nassau County courts, from arraignment through to disposition. A good number of these begin as something smaller: a traffic stop, a dispute, a night that got away from someone.

Knowing the room

Criminal practice in a county you know is different from criminal practice in a county you do not. Jan D. Goldman has appeared in these courts since 1982, and that matters in the ordinary, unglamorous ways: knowing how a particular court schedules, what a particular prosecutor will and will not consider, and how long a realistic answer takes to arrive.

You will be told what you are charged with in plain English, what the exposure is, and what the likely paths are. If the honest answer is unwelcome, you will still get it.

Negligence Matters

Claims brought on behalf of people injured through someone else's carelessness, most often in motor vehicle collisions.

What to do first

  • Get treated, and keep every record of it
  • Photograph the vehicles, the scene and your injuries while they are visible
  • Take the names and numbers of witnesses before they leave
  • Report the collision as your policy requires
  • Do not give a recorded statement to the other side's insurer before you have advice

New York has strict time limits for bringing a claim, and shorter ones still where a public body is involved. A claim that is plainly good can be lost on a date alone, so the sooner the facts are looked at, the better.
